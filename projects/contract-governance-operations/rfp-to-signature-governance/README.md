# RFP-to-Signature Governance | Risk-Based Decision & Approval Framework

**Contract Governance | RFP / Proposal Operations | Stage-Gate Reviews | Risk Management | Cross-Functional Accountability**

## Overview

RFP and contract decisions can become fragmented when Sales, Finance, Legal, Compliance and Program Management enter the process at different times or review different pieces of the opportunity.

I developed a structured, Shipley-informed stage-gate framework to bring the right internal stakeholders into the process at the right time, create clear decision points, and make financial, contractual, compliance and delivery risk visible before the organization commits to an opportunity.

The framework follows the opportunity from RFP intake through signature and contract handoff.

## Business need

The goal was to create a repeatable process that could:
- streamline pursue / do-not-pursue and approval decisions;
- clarify who is accountable for each type of review;
- surface financial, legal, compliance and delivery risks earlier;
- reduce late-stage surprises and rework;
- scale the level of review based on contract value, complexity and risk; and
- create a cleaner handoff from pre-award activity into post-award contract launch.

## Approach

I structured the process around six stages:

1. **RFP Intake & Initial Assessment** — confirm strategic fit, target market and core capability alignment.
2. **Opportunity Evaluation** — assess requirements, customer, scope, competitive landscape, risk and viability.
3. **Solution Development & Internal Review** — develop the solution, pricing, implementation approach and contract strategy.
4. **Final Review & Approval to Submit** — confirm proposal, pricing and contract positions are ready for submission.
5. **Negotiation & Final Review** — resolve commercial, legal, compliance and operational issues and obtain final terms approval.
6. **Signature & Handoff** — confirm required approvals, execute the agreement and initiate contract launch.

Each stage has a defined gate so the opportunity either proceeds, is reworked or escalated, or is not pursued.

## Cross-functional accountability

- **Sales** — customer strategy, solution approach and win plan.
- **Program Management** — delivery feasibility, resources, implementation and operational readiness.
- **Finance** — financial viability, pricing, margin, cost structure and contract-value considerations.
- **Legal** — contract terms, redlines, negotiation support, legal risk and signature authority.
- **Compliance** — regulatory, customer, ethical, policy, data-security and other applicable requirements.

The level of involvement is not intended to be identical for every opportunity. Contract value, complexity, non-standard terms, regulatory requirements, strategic importance and delivery risk can all increase the level of review or approval required.

## Governance principle

> **Right-size the review to the value, risk and complexity of the opportunity.**

A lower-risk opportunity using standard terms may need a lighter review path. A higher-value or more complex opportunity may require Finance, Legal, Compliance, Program Management and executive involvement much earlier.

The contract-value bands in the supporting artifact are intentionally shown as placeholders (`$X` and `$Y`) so an organization can apply its own approval authority and delegation thresholds.

## How this reduces risk

The framework creates earlier visibility into questions such as:
- Can the organization operationally deliver what is being proposed?
- Is the pricing and financial exposure acceptable?
- Are non-standard contract positions understood and accepted?
- Are compliance or regulatory requirements known before submission?
- Have the appropriate stakeholders accepted the risks within their area of responsibility?
- Is the final agreement consistent with what was approved during the RFP process?

## Operational impact

This type of governance supports more consistent decision-making, clearer ownership and accountability, earlier identification and mitigation of risk, fewer late-stage approval surprises, better alignment between Sales and delivery teams, and a more controlled transition from RFP response to executed contract and post-award launch.

No quantified performance improvement is claimed in this representative sample; the purpose is to demonstrate the operating model and governance design.
